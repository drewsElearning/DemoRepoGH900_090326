# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

The **[Repository or Community Name]** community is committed to providing a welcoming, respectful, inclusive, and productive learning environment for everyone.

This repository is intended for GH-900 learners, instructors, contributors, reviewers, and maintainers to practice GitHub skills and collaborate professionally.

Participation is open to people of all backgrounds, identities, experience levels, abilities, and perspectives.

By participating in this repository, you agree to follow this Code of Conduct in all associated project and community spaces.

---

## Expected Behavior

Examples of positive and constructive behavior include:

- Treating others with respect, patience, and empathy.
- Using welcoming, inclusive, and professional language.
- Respecting different viewpoints, experiences, and skill levels.
- Giving feedback that is specific, helpful, and focused on the work.
- Receiving constructive feedback professionally.
- Asking questions when instructions or feedback are unclear.
- Keeping issues, pull requests, reviews, comments, and discussions relevant to the project.
- Allowing learners to make mistakes and supporting them as they build new skills.
- Crediting the work and ideas of others when appropriate.
- Protecting confidential, personal, and sensitive information.
- Following repository contribution, security, and licensing guidance.
- Reporting security concerns through the process described in `SECURITY.md`.
- Accepting responsibility for mistakes, correcting them, and learning from the experience.

---

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors are not acceptable:

- Harassment, intimidation, bullying, threats, or discrimination.
- Insults, personal attacks, hostile comments, or deliberately disruptive behavior.
- Repeatedly dismissing or belittling another person's questions, contributions, or experience.
- Sharing another person's private or identifying information without permission.
- Posting confidential organizational, customer, learner, or project information.
- Committing passwords, tokens, keys, credentials, or other secrets.
- Submitting malicious code, intentionally harmful links, or misleading content.
- Impersonating another person or misrepresenting your identity or authority.
- Spamming issues, pull requests, discussions, or review threads.
- Continuing unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Retaliating against someone who raises a concern in good faith.
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional learning or collaboration environment.

---

## Constructive Feedback Guidelines

When reviewing work or participating in a discussion:

1. Focus on the content, code, or proposed change rather than the person.
2. Explain why a change may be helpful.
3. Offer a clear suggestion when possible.
4. Distinguish required changes from optional recommendations.
5. Ask questions instead of making assumptions.
6. Recognize useful contributions and improvements.
7. Keep feedback professional and relevant to the project.

### Feedback Example

Instead of writing:

> This is wrong.

Consider writing:

> Could we update this example to use a relative image path? That may make the image more reliable when learners fork the repository.

## Review and Enforcement

Repository maintainers are responsible for applying this Code of Conduct consistently, fairly, and in good faith.

Depending on the nature, severity, and history of the behavior, maintainers may take actions such as:

1. Providing a private clarification or reminder.
2. Requesting that content or behavior be corrected.
3. Removing or editing comments, commits, issues, pull requests, or other contributions.
4. Temporarily limiting participation.
5. Permanently removing access to project spaces.
6. Escalating the concern through the appropriate organizational or platform process when required.

When appropriate, maintainers should explain moderation decisions to the affected participant while protecting the privacy and safety of everyone involved.

## Appeals

A participant may request a review of an enforcement decision by contacting:

- **Appeals contact:** [Appeals Contact or Team]

Whenever practical, the appeal should be reviewed by someone who was not the sole decision-maker in the original action.

## Maintainer Responsibilities

Maintainers are expected to:

- Model respectful and professional behavior.
- Respond to reports without unnecessary delay.
- Avoid conflicts of interest when reviewing concerns.
- Document moderation decisions appropriately.
- Protect the privacy and security of reporters and participants.
- Apply expectations consistently across contributors.
- Avoid retaliation and address retaliation if it occurs.
- Periodically review this document.
- Keep reporting and contact information current.

---

## Security and Privacy

All participants must help protect the repository and its community.

Do not commit or publicly share:

- Passwords
- Access tokens
- API keys
- Private keys
- Connection strings
- Customer information
- Confidential organizational information
- Personally identifiable information
- Private learner records

Use sample, fictional, or sanitized information during exercises and demonstrations.

Potential security vulnerabilities should be reported privately according to the instructions in `SECURITY.md`, not through a public issue.
